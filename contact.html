FAQ

Questions clients ask before starting

Straight answers about the workflow, files and what is and is not included.

Can you develop an electronic product from just an idea?

Yes. You can start with an idea, a rough sketch, reference images, functional requirements or a full specification. I analyse the requirements, select the microcontroller and components, plan the hardware architecture and then take the product through firmware, breadboard prototype, testing, schematic, PCB layout and enclosure.

What is your product development workflow?

Eight stages: (1) idea and requirements, (2) firmware development, (3) breadboard hardware, (4) testing and debugging, (5) KiCad schematic, (6) PCB design, (7) custom 3D enclosure, and (8) engineering handover. You can see it on the process section and in Complete Product Development.

Do you develop firmware before PCB design?

Yes. My approach is firmware-first: I make the product work on real hardware before committing to a board. That way the PCB is designed around behaviour that has already been validated, not around assumptions.

Do you test the hardware on a breadboard?

Yes. After the firmware is written I build the circuit on a breadboard with the actual components, load the firmware and test and debug it before moving to the schematic and PCB. If you already have a working prototype, I start from it instead.

Do you provide KiCad schematic and PCB files?

Yes. The handover includes the editable KiCad schematic and PCB project, Gerber files, drill files and a bill of materials. KiCad is my default tool. When your project already lives in Altium Designer or EasyEDA Pro, I can work in those as well.

Do you provide complete firmware source code?

Yes. For firmware I develop for your product, you receive the complete source code, together with the wiring diagram and testing documentation so the work can be reproduced and extended.

Do you design custom 3D enclosures?

Yes. I design the enclosure around the actual PCB and your product requirements: mounting points, connector openings, display and button cutouts, with STEP and STL exports. The Beacon enclosure is a five-part example.

Do you manufacture PCBs?

No. I do not provide PCB manufacturing, printing or assembly. I deliver fabrication-ready files, and you order the boards from the manufacturer of your choice. I have prepared outputs for JLCPCB, PCBWay and OSHPark.

Do you provide enclosure 3D printing?

No. I provide print-ready STL and STEP files with suggested print notes. You print the parts yourself or through a printing service of your choice.

Who supplies the hardware components?

You do. Clients either supply the components or cover the purchasing cost after approval. I do not purchase components at my own expense, and I do not ship physical products.

Will I receive editable engineering files?

Yes. Depending on the scope, that means the firmware source, wiring diagrams, KiCad schematic and PCB project files, Gerber and drill files, BOM, and the 3D enclosure CAD files with STEP and STL exports, so you are never locked in.

Can you work on existing projects?

Yes. I can join at the stage that fits: layout from a finished schematic (the multi-radio gateway), rebuilding and repairing an undocumented board (ARC62), redesigning a board (RejsaCAN) or designing an enclosure around a supplied PCB.

How do you calculate project pricing?

Projects are quoted by scope, after I have reviewed your requirements: the number of stages involved, the complexity of the firmware and hardware, board size and layer count, and whether an enclosure is needed. There are no fixed packages. The budget field in the inquiry form is optional and only helps me size the proposal.

How are revisions handled?

Work is delivered in milestones and reviewed with you before the next stage begins. Change requests are handled in those review rounds, and the number of revision rounds is agreed in the project scope before work starts. On the parcel-tracking system, every schematic was reviewed with the client before layout.

Can you support me after PCB fabrication?

Yes, within the scope we agree. I can answer questions about the delivered files and help investigate anything found when your fabricated boards arrive. Because I do not manufacture or assemble boards, I do not claim physical test results for a fabricated PCB unless I was given the hardware to test.
